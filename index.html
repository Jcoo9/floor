The Sales Management Gap

A Sales Management Gap happens when a company has enough salespeople to require active management, but nobody consistently owns the team's pipeline, coaching, accountability, forecasting and performance.

It usually shows up quietly. The company hires a few sellers, growth outpaces the founder's attention, and management becomes whatever's left over after everything else. Nobody decided to leave the team unmanaged — it just happened while everyone was busy. More on this soon in Insights →

Before you call

A few things founders ask me first.

Will my sellers push back on being managed by someone outside the company?

Usually the opposite. Sellers who aren't being managed at all tend to want structure — clear expectations, real coaching, someone who notices when a deal stalls. I run the cadence the way an internal manager would: I'm in the pipeline meetings, on the 1:1s, and accountable for the number. Not watching from the outside.

How much of my time does this actually take?

Less than you're spending managing the team yourself right now. You'll get a regular, defined update from me instead of carrying the pipeline meeting, the coaching and the follow-up personally. My job is to take that off your plate, not add a second meeting to your week.

What happens if the Floor Check says I don't need ongoing management?

You still keep the diagnosis and the 30-day plan — that's what you paid for. I built the Floor Check to give you an honest answer, not to funnel you into a retainer. If the right move is a different hire, a process fix, or nothing at all, I'll tell you that.

Do you replace my salespeople, or manage the ones I have?

I manage the team you already have. I don't recruit or replace sellers — if the Floor Check turns up a gap that's really a hiring problem, I'll tell you that directly instead of trying to solve it myself.
