The Revenue Leak Check

12 places founder-led companies quietly lose revenue.

Most companies assume they need more leads. Sometimes they do. But before spending more money to create demand, it is worth checking whether revenue is already leaking out of the business.

This is not a formal assessment. It is a quick gut check.

Start the check
For each one, pick what feels true

No issue. Sometimes. Yep, this is us.

01

Deals are sitting around

How many deals are in the pipeline right now that everybody knows probably are not moving? If there is no clear next step, date or owner, they are not really pipeline. They are clutter.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
02

Old customers have gone quiet

Do you have former customers nobody has meaningfully reached back out to in the last 6–12 months? There is often easier revenue sitting in old relationships than in brand-new leads.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
03

Good customers could be buying more

Do you have current customers who probably have more needs than what they buy from you today? If nobody owns cross-sell or account growth, those opportunities usually sit there.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
04

Follow-up is inconsistent

Does follow-up depend too much on people remembering what they said they would do? A lot of revenue dies because nobody followed up at the right time.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
05

The founder is still in too many deals

Do too many important opportunities still need the founder to step in, rescue them, approve them or close them? That works for a while. Then it becomes a bottleneck.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
06

The sales team is active, but not really managed

Are people selling, but nobody is consistently running pipeline, priorities, coaching, accountability and next actions? Activity is not the same as management.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
07

Nobody really trusts the pipeline

Can you quickly tell which deals are likely to close and which ones are fantasy? If not, the company may be spending too much time on the wrong opportunities.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
08

Partnerships get talked about more than pursued

Do you have potential referral partners, channel partners, distributors or strategic relationships that never really go anywhere? If everybody owns partnerships, usually nobody owns partnerships.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
09

Good revenue ideas never get launched

Do you talk about new offers, packages, services, sponsorships, products or ways to monetize what you already have — and then nothing happens?

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
10

There are obvious buyers nobody is going after

Are there customer types, industries, geographies or buyer groups you could probably sell to that nobody has really pursued?

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
11

Pricing and packaging have not changed in forever

Are you selling roughly the same way you always have? Sometimes the opportunity is not more volume. It is a better package, better structure, better pricing or a clearer reason to buy.

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us
12

AI got added before the sales process got fixed

Are AI tools being dropped into the business without anyone first deciding what humans should own, what machines should own and how the work actually gets done?

No issue Sometimes Yep, this is us

So, what did you find?

If you checked “Yep, this is us” more than a few times, the problem may not be lead volume. It may be that nobody is really owning the whole revenue picture.

Mostly “No issue”Your commercial operation may be healthier than you think. Look for one or two isolated leaks rather than rebuilding everything.
A lot of “Sometimes”You probably have inconsistent execution. These are the places where revenue quietly gets lost over time.
Several “Yep, this is us”You may not need more leads first. You may need someone to get inside the business and start moving what is already there.
