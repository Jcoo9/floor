Fractional VP of Sales vs. Full-Time: Which Does Your Company Need?

Short answer: A fractional VP of Sales makes sense when a company needs senior sales leadership and structure but isn't ready for the cost or multi-month search that comes with a full-time executive hire. A full-time VP makes sense once the team, revenue, and org complexity justify a permanent, dedicated seat.

What fractional sales leadership solves

Fractional leadership gives a company senior judgment — on pipeline, forecasting, hiring, and structure — without the full-time cost or the multi-month executive search. It's well suited to companies that need the thinking of a senior leader but not yet the full org-building scope of the role.

What only a full-time hire solves

Some things genuinely need someone in the seat full-time: building and managing a growing sales org day-to-day, being available for every deal escalation, and being a permanent, dedicated face to the board and to the sales team. Fractional leadership isn't a substitute for that once a company reaches that scale.

Cost and commitment differences

A full-time VP of Sales hire typically runs well into six figures in base compensation alone, plus a multi-month search and onboarding runway. Fractional engagements are structured monthly, without the long-term compensation and equity commitment of a permanent executive hire.

A practical way to decide

If the honest answer to 'do we need someone in this seat five days a week, permanently, right now' is no — but the team still needs senior sales leadership — fractional is usually the better first step, with the option to convert to full-time once the company's stage justifies it.
